Mathematical Modeling of Hepatic Circadian Rhythms

Abstract

Circadian rhythms are endogenous 24-hour oscillations that regulate physiological processes in the body. The circadian rhythm of peripheral clocks is regulated by the suprachiasmatic nucleus, which uses light as external input to synchronise cellular rhythms throughout the body. The liver peripheral clock is unique, as it is entrained largely by inputs from feeding habits, which often are prioritised over photic inputs from the SCN. Additionally, the liver core clock is also influenced by the HPA (hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal) axis, or the stress response. The HPA axis is in crosstalk with the inflammatory response through the action of glucocorticoids (the most common of which is cortisol). GCs act as entrainers for the liver core clock by regulating the expression of core clock genes through their promoters. Hence, the HPA-inflammation axis, along with glucocorticoid receptors, allows homeostasis in the circadian rhythm of peripheral systems. Improper synchronisation may lead to chronic diseases such as diabetes and metabolic syndrome. To study this system, we have adopted a computational analysis of mathematical models simulating the individual modules. The modules were integrated to study the complex interconnections between them. Here, we aim to validate and use this integrated model to analyse the effects of different dietary patterns and habits, including meal timings and meal compositions, on the circadian rhythm of the liver core clock.

Objectives

1. Literature review of the crosstalk between dietary behaviour (feeding patterns and meal compositions), the HPA axis, the inflammatory module, and the hepatic core clock. 2. Optimisation and validation of the integrated computational model against the reported human circadian physiology of the liver core clock. 3. To systematically simulate the perturbations of the feeding cycle (specifically diet composition and timing) on the hepatic core clock. 4. By studying these perturbations in the peripheral liver core clock, the effects of meal timing and meal composition on circadian dysregulation and associated metabolic disorders can be studied.
